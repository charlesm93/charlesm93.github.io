## Applying to our graduate programs

_I'm actively recruiting PhD and MSc students to join my research group._

Admissions are handled by the department, rather than by me individually,
so the best way to reach me is to 
[apply to one of our graduate programs at UBC](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/graduate-admissions).
I'll review the application of qualified candidates who mention in their
application that they are interested in working with me.

Note that students are admitted to the program rather than to a specific
research group and students typically have a year before committing to an
advisor. In your application, it is acceptable to express interest in multiple
potential advisors and in some cases, it can be natural to be co-advised by
two faculty members.

_Sending a separate email will not affect the outcome of your application. 
I currently receive more inquiries than I can respond to. Even if I don't 
respond to your email, you still receive full consideration when you submit an
application._

## If you are a graduate student on campus...

...in statistics or in another field,  you can reach out to me to discuss 
research opportunities and collaborations. 

If I'm teaching a graduate class, enrolling and doing a final
project in that class is a great way to connect.


## If you are an undergraduate student on campus...

Currently, I do not have specific opportunities for undergraduate students,
however I'm open to working with an exceptional candidate over the summer.

I encourage undergraduate students to take courses with a final project requirement.
Final projects are meant to get students working on an open-ended question
(which is exactly what research is), give you a framework to get feedback
from faculty and provide a great way to assess mutual fit.

If you're interested in the type of work I do, I encourage you take
_STAT 405: Bayesian Statistics_.
